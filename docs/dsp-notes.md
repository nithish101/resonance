# DSP notes

How each feature is computed, why the parameters are what they are, and how each piece is
verified. Code: `crates/dsp/src`. Everything runs once per hop with no heap allocation
(`tests/no_alloc.rs` installs a counting global allocator and asserts zero allocations across
thousands of hops at 44.1 and 48 kHz).

## 1. Framing (STFT)

- Mono downmix in the AudioWorklet (average of channels).
- Frame N = 2048 samples, hop H = 512 (75% overlap), periodic Hann window
  w[i] = 0.5 - 0.5·cos(2πi/N). At 48 kHz that's a 42.7 ms window, 23.4 Hz bins, and 93.75
  frames per second.
- Every frequency-dependent parameter (band edges, mel filters, tempo lags, envelope time
  constants) is derived from the actual sample rate (IN-7), so 44.1 and 48 kHz give the same
  results in physical units. Tests run both rates.

Verified against librosa's window (sum = N/2 exactly; constant overlap-add at N/4).

## 2. FFT (hand-written)

`fft.rs` has two transforms:

- **Complex radix-2, iterative, in place.** Bit-reversal permutation, then log2(N) butterfly
  stages. Twiddles are precomputed in f64 and stored **stage by stage**, so each stage's inner
  loop reads them contiguously, with a separate conjugate table for the inverse. The first
  version indexed one table with a stride and branched on direction inside the loop; the
  per-stage layout made it 1.5x faster.
- **Real-input FFT via a half-size complex FFT** (P1 item, done early because it's the hot path).
  Pack z[j] = x[2j] + i·x[2j+1], run an N/2 complex FFT, then untangle:
  E[k] = (Z[k] + conj Z[N/2-k]) / 2 (even samples), O[k] = (Z[k] - conj Z[N/2-k]) / 2i (odd
  samples), X[k] = E[k] + e^(-2πik/N)·O[k]. Half the work of a full-size complex FFT on zero
  imaginary parts.

Verified (`tests/fft.rs`): a sine lands in its bin with magnitude N/2; agreement with `rustfft`
below 1e-4 relative error for every size from 2 to 16384 (complex) and 4 to 16384 (real);
Parseval's theorem; linearity; and a proptest round-trip `inverse(forward(x)) == x` over random
sizes and signals. Speed against rustfft is in [benchmarks.md](benchmarks.md).

## 3. Power spectrum and mel filterbank

Power is normalized so a full-scale sine reads 0 dB in its peak bin:
P[k] = |X[k]|² / (Σw / 2)².

The 64-band mel filterbank uses the **Slaney** (Auditory Toolbox) mel scale (linear below 1 kHz,
logarithmic above: m = 15 + 27·ln(f/1000)/ln 6.4) with triangles normalized to unit area in Hz
(norm = 2 / (f_{m+2} - f_m)), from 30 Hz to min(16 kHz, Nyquist). Filters are stored sparsely
(start bin + weights).

Verified (`tests/mel.rs`): the mel scale and **every weight of every filter** match
`librosa.filters.mel(htk=False, norm="slaney")` (librosa 1.0) at 44.1 and 48 kHz within 1e-6
relative; the reference data is generated by a script and committed. Filter areas integrate to 1.

## 4. Bands, envelopes, auto-gain, noise gate (DSP-4, ER-4)

- Six bands sum power over fixed Hz ranges (20-60, 60-250, 250-500, 500-2k, 2k-4k, 4k-16k Hz).
- **Auto-gain**: each band (and each mel bin) has a slow peak tracker in dB that jumps up
  instantly and decays at 3 dB/s. The displayed value is
  `clamp((level - (reference - range)) / range, 0, 1)` with `range = 36 dB / sensitivity`. The
  reference is capped at 30 dB below the loudest band's reference, so an empty band can't be
  boosted into noise.
- **Envelope followers**: instant attack, release 40-540 ms depending on the smoothing setting.
- **Noise gate**: the frame's RMS level opens the gate above -57 dBFS and closes it below
  -62 dBFS (hysteresis). While closed, the auto-gain references stop adapting (and never fall
  below -48 dBFS anyway), onsets are suppressed, and every displayed value fades to zero with a
  250 ms release.

Why: quiet and loud masters should both fill the visuals, but silence must stay black, not
amplified room noise flickering at full brightness.

Verified (`tests/analyzer.rs`): a 1 kHz sine lights the "mid" band and gives a 1 kHz centroid at
both sample rates; silence and a -70 dBFS noise floor produce exactly zero output and no onsets;
the same groove at 0 dB and -26 dB gives band and loudness values within 0.1 of each other; a
track stopping fades out without single-frame jumps.

## 5. Onsets (DSP-5)

Spectral flux on the log-mel spectrum:

1. L_b = 10·log10(mel power), floored per bin at 40 dB below that bin's own slow peak.
2. flux_t = mean over bins of max(0, L_b(t) - L_b(t-1)) (half-wave rectified rise).
3. Threshold_t = median of the previous 32 flux values (~0.35 s) + δ, with δ = 1.2 dB /
   sensitivity.
4. Frame t-1 is an onset if flux(t-1) > threshold(t-1), it's a local maximum
   (flux(t-1) ≥ flux(t-2) and > flux(t)), and at least 60 ms have passed since the last onset.
   The decision needs flux(t), so onsets are reported one hop late, attributed to t-1.

**The per-bin floor** was the one real tuning problem. Without a floor, low-level beating between
sustained partials registered as rises (precision 0.81 on the synthetic groove). A single floor
relative to the loudest bin fixed that but silenced the hi-hats, because Slaney area
normalization puts wide high-frequency bins on a much lower dB scale (recall 0.63). Flooring each
bin relative to its own history fixed both (F = 0.98). This was tuned on synthetic data;
[evaluation.md](evaluation.md) also reports humanized grooves that weren't used for tuning.

## 6. Tempo (DSP-6)

Autocorrelation of the onset-strength envelope, every 8 hops:

1. Take the last 512 flux values (about 5.5-6 s), remove the mean, zero-pad to 1024.
2. ACF via FFT: |FFT|², inverse FFT, normalized by lag 0. This is the _biased_ ACF, which decays
   with lag.
3. Score each candidate tempo on a 0.25 BPM grid from 60 to 180 BPM:
   S(b) = Σ_{k=1..4} acf(k·L_b) / k, with L_b = 60·hop_rate / b in frames, linearly interpolated
   between integer lags. Summing the first four multiples gives sub-frame precision, since an
   error at 4L is a quarter of the error at L.
4. Multiply by a log-Gaussian prior centred at 120 BPM with σ = 1 octave:
   w(b) = exp(-½·(log2(b/120))²).
5. Refine the best grid point with a parabola through its neighbours.
6. Hysteresis: a candidate more than 4% from the current estimate must win three updates in a
   row before replacing it; nearby estimates are blended (0.7/0.3).

**Why this avoids octave errors.** For a pulse train with period P, the ACF has peaks at every
multiple of P. The candidate P (true tempo) and 2P (half tempo) both hit peaks at all four
multiples, but the biased ACF decays with lag, so P scores higher. The candidate P/2 (double
tempo) only hits peaks at even multiples, so it scores about half. The prior breaks the
remaining near-ties toward the 90-150 BPM range where most music sits. On paper, and in tests,
this picks the right octave for 70, 90, 120, 140, and 174 BPM.

Verified (`tests/tempo.rs`): click tracks at 70, 90, 120, 140, and 174 BPM land within 0.5 BPM at
both sample rates (the PRD asks for ±2), with no half/double errors; grooves are exact; a jump
from 100 to 130 BPM is followed within 8 s; silence reports no tempo. On the bundled house tracks
the estimates are stable at 122, 126, and 123 BPM (not ground truth; see the eval caveats).

## 7. Centroid and loudness (DSP-10)

- Spectral centroid = Σ f_k·|X_k| / Σ |X_k| (magnitude-weighted), plus a log-normalized 0..1
  version for shaders.
- Loudness = RMS level in dB, auto-gained against the same slow peak tracker as the bands, gated.

## 8. Beat tracking (DSP-7)

`beat.rs`. Tempo says how often beats come; the tracker decides when. Every 4 hops it scores a
comb of impulses one period apart over the last ~6 s of a **bass-weighted** onset envelope
(0.35 × all-band flux + 0.65 × flux of the mel bands below 200 Hz), with weights 0.8^k for older
impulses, and takes the best offset as "the last beat was o frames ago". That measurement steers
a phase-locked loop: errors under 20% of a period nudge the predicted next beat by 35% of the
error; a larger error must repeat twice before the tracker jumps. Beats fire when the frame clock
reaches the _predicted_ time, so they land on the beat instead of a detection delay after it.

**Why bass-weighted.** With all-band flux, the tracker locked half a beat off on every humanized
groove (F = 0): broadband off-beat hi-hats rise across many mel bands, while the kick lights only
a few low ones. Weighting the low bands fixed it (F 0.98-0.99). Tempo still uses the all-band
flux: when it used the bass-weighted envelope too, bass-line rhythms pulled a real house track to
2/3 of its tempo (81 instead of 122 BPM).

## 9. Offline beat grid (DSP-9)

`beatgrid.rs`: Ellis's 2007 dynamic-programming beat tracker (the algorithm behind librosa's
`beat_track`), over the whole file's bass-weighted onset envelope with the median tempo:
C(t) = O(t) + max over τ in [t - 2P, t - P/2] of (C(τ) - 100 · log²((t - τ)/P)), then a backtrack
from the best final beat, then trimming weak beats at the edges. Non-causal, so it runs once per
file in a worker (`workers/offline.worker.ts`) on a 22.05 kHz mono decode, about 2 s for a 4:43
track on the build machine. The renderer uses this grid in file mode, so beat-synced visuals
anticipate the beat instead of reacting to it; the seek bar draws the energy envelope with a tick
every four beats.

## 10. Chroma and key (DSP-8)

`chroma.rs`. Each FFT bin from **200 Hz** to 5 kHz adds its magnitude to its nearest pitch class
(MIDI 69 + 12·log2(f/440), mod 12); the vector is normalized and smoothed (~200 ms). The floor
matters: a 2048-point FFT's bins are wider than a semitone below about 400 Hz, and with a 55 Hz
floor the kick drum's energy landed on C♯ and dominated every frame, turning a C-major groove into
B♭ minor. Chord tones still register through their harmonics above 200 Hz.

Key: a slow (~8 s) chroma average is correlated (Pearson) with the Krumhansl-Kessler major and
minor profiles rotated to all 12 tonics; the best of 24 wins, and confidence is how far it clears
the runner-up. The "Follow the key" colour theme maps keys around the circle of fifths, so keys
a fifth apart get neighbouring hues and minor keys are darker.

Verified (`tests/beat_chroma.rs`): online beats F ≥ 0.989 on click tracks at 70-174 BPM and
0.977 on a humanized groove; offline grid F = 1.000 on humanized grooves at 96, 128, and 150 BPM;
C-major and A-minor triads peak on their chord tones; C-major and A-minor scales get the right
key; every rotated profile classifies as itself; the C-major groove is detected as C major.
